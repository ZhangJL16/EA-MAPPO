## Exclusions

- MDPI was excluded by policy.
- Wikipedia, ResearchGate, alphaXiv, generic blogs, and scraped mirrors were not
  used as final evidence.
- Papers about generic stationary MCMC, quantum estimation, or finite-horizon
  control without a killed/absorbing/risk/statistical collision were screened
  out.
- Quasi-stationary Monte Carlo papers focused only on constructing an MCMC target
  were retained only when they supplied eigenvalue or convergence theory.

## Source-Supported Findings

- Markov-chain sensitivity can be bounded through eigenvalue/conditioning
  quantities, including nearly transient cases.
- Killed-process perturbation theory controls principal eigenfunctions and QSDs.
- Feynman--Kac particle work establishes nonasymptotic variance, sharp first-order
  expansions, and some joint horizon--particle CLTs.
- QSD stochastic approximation has a multidimensional CLT and explicit
  eigenvalue-related slow-convergence examples.
- SSP learning has matching or near-matching sample-complexity bounds involving
  hitting-time/cost scales.
- Margin assumptions and minimax plug-in decision rates are classical.
- Adaptive variance-optimal stratum allocation, heterogeneous acquisition costs,
  pilot-estimated oracle designs, and rare-event trajectory stratification are
  established independently of this project.
- For a general nonnormal matrix with a simple eigenvalue, first-order left/right
  eigenvector perturbation via eigenprojectors and related group-inverse formulas
  is established independently of this project.
- Online unknown-variance stratified sampling has oracle-regret upper bounds and
  matching-order lower bounds, while empirical Bernstein bounds provide
  classical bounded sample-variance concentration.
- Shared allocations for multiple characteristics have been posed as convex or
  integer nonlinear programs for decades. Efficient OPE with multiple logger
  strata and behavior-policy search further occupy generic shared-data and
  active-OPE claims.

## Searcher Inferences

- The screened papers do not advertise the exact conditional-primitive canonical
  gradient in Theorem 18 or the coefficient \(\mathcal C_0\) coupling quotient
  source coverage with right-Perron projected conditional noise.
- The projected-noise-degenerate exponent split appears less directly occupied
  than the generic spectral blow-up, but this remains an opportunity hypothesis.
- The cost-aware oracle and generic adaptive plug-in allocation are occupied.
  The narrower opportunity is the cancellation of the critical \(1/\Delta\)
  factor from normalized source priorities, and the resulting separation between
  pilot design consistency and risk-value resolution.
- Theorem 23's reduced-resolvent and empirical-moment arguments are proof tools.
  The narrower opportunity is their simultaneous appearance with the critical
  approximation condition \(\Delta/\sigma_*^2\), directional anisotropy
  \(\chi\), quotient bias, and a stopped-decision margin.
- A shared multi-query allocation is not new by itself. The defensible object
  would be the stopped-margin-powered objective composed with critical Perron
  sensitivities, plus singularity cancellation and adaptive failure conditions.
