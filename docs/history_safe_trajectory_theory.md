# History-Conditioned Physics-Guided Safe Trajectory Theory

## Target

Derive the strongest correct statement supported by a pipeline in which history-conditioned mechanisms propose finite control sequences, the current UAV dynamics roll them out, and an exact continuous-time verifier decides safety. The target is not a theorem that a neural proposal is safe. The invariant object is the **true executed trajectory under clipped, sampled-data dynamics**.

## Status

**COHERENT AFTER REFRAMING / EXTRA ASSUMPTION.** Finite-horizon trajectory safety, certified coarse rejection, terminal-set recursive feasibility, conditional CLF progress, and candidate-set energy optimality are coherent. A novel deterministic history-adaptive tube is not established. Generic recursive-feasibility and sampling-suboptimality statements are known MPC/sampling structures and are not claimed as novel.

## 1. Definitions

At decision time \(t\), the causal history is

\[
H_t=\{x_{t-L:t},u_{t-L:t-1},z_{t-L:t}\},\qquad L\in\{2,4,8,16\}.
\]

It contains only measurements and executed controls available no later than \(t\). The proposal distribution is

\[
q(U\mid H_t,x_t,u_{\mathrm{SAC},t},z_t),\quad
U=(u_0,\ldots,u_{H-1}).
\]

The clipped sampled-data physics map is

\[
\tau(U)=\Phi(x_t,U),
\]

where each physics subinterval has duration \(\delta\), commanded acceleration is clipped to the input set, velocity is clipped to the physical speed set, realized propulsion acceleration is

\[
a_k^{\rm real}=\frac{v_{k+1}^{\rm prop}-v_k}{\delta},
\]

and position is propagated by

\[
p_{k+1}=p_k+v_k\delta+\tfrac12a_k^{\rm real}\delta^2.
\]

Boundary projection is applied after propulsion-energy accounting and is not treated as propulsion acceleration.

For obstacle \(i\), safe distance \(d_i\), and relative path

\[
r_{k,i}(s)=p_k-c_{k,i}+(v_k-v_{i,k})s+\tfrac12(a_k^{\rm real}-a_{i,k})s^2,
\quad s\in[0,\delta],
\]

define the interval barrier

\[
h_{k,i}(s)=\|r_{k,i}(s)\|_2^2-d_i^2.
\]

The finite candidate certificate is

\[
\mathcal T_{\rm cert}(x_t)=\{U:\min_{k,i,s\in[0,\delta]}h_{k,i}(s)\ge0,
\ U\in\mathcal U^H,\ p(s)\in\mathcal W\}.
\]

The progress-admissible subset is

\[
\mathcal T_{\rm prog}(x_t)=\{U\in\mathcal T_{\rm cert}(x_t):
V_g(x_H)\le V_g(x_0)-\Delta_V\},
\]

with

\[
V_g(p,v)=\tfrac12\|p-p_g\|_2^2+\tfrac\beta2\|v-v_g\|_2^2.
\]

The evaluated energy is

\[
J_H(U)=\sum_{k=0}^{H-1}c_{\rm telemetry}(x_k,a_k^{\rm real})
+\widehat E_{\rm terminal}^{\rm upper}(x_H,g).
\]

## 2. Assumptions

- **A1 (causality):** proposal inputs contain no data after decision time \(t\).
- **A2 (rollout fidelity):** the rollout and deployed transition use the same action clipping, speed clipping, integration order, physics time step, and boundary semantics.
- **A3 (obstacle model for deterministic certification):** each obstacle trajectory over the candidate horizon is known exactly or enclosed by an explicitly supplied deterministic set.
- **A4 (exact interval solver):** every real stationary point of the quartic barrier on each closed hold interval is evaluated, in addition to endpoints.
- **A5 (terminal estimator scope):** the energy estimator is frozen during selection; an upper label is a statistical energy object, not a collision certificate.
- **A6 (backup result only):** a backup set \(\mathcal C_B\) and backup controller \(\kappa_B\) are available, forward invariant, and compatible with the same obstacle assumptions.
- **A7 (cover result only):** the feasible action-sequence set is compact and \(J_H\) is \(L_J\)-Lipschitz in the selected norm.

## 3. Lemma 1 — Rollout Consistency

**Claim.** Under A2, for a fixed initial state and fixed action sequence, scalar and batched implementations of \(\Phi\) produce the same states, realized accelerations, and telemetry energy up to numerical roundoff.

**Reason.** Every candidate applies the same deterministic sequence of componentwise/cylindrical projections and algebraic updates. Batching changes the evaluation order, not the map. This is an implementation-equivalence lemma, not a model-validity theorem.

## 4. Theorem 1 — Exact Finite-Horizon Trajectory Safety

**Claim.** Under A2–A4, if \(U\in\mathcal T_{\rm cert}(x_t)\), then the true rollout generated by \(\Phi(x_t,U)\) is collision-free and remains inside the certified world boundary for the full horizon, including times between policy samples.

**Proof.** On each physics hold, \(r_{k,i}(s)\) is quadratic and \(h_{k,i}(s)\) is quartic. The minimum of a differentiable polynomial on a compact interval occurs at an endpoint or a real root of its derivative. A4 evaluates all such times. Therefore the reported interval minimum is the true interval minimum. Nonnegative minima for all obstacle/interval pairs imply \(h_{k,i}(s)\ge0\) everywhere. The boundary verifier applies the same argument coordinatewise or rejects any projected rollout. Taking the conjunction over intervals proves finite-horizon safety. ∎

**Boundary.** This theorem ends at the candidate horizon. It does not imply recursive feasibility without a terminal condition.

## 5. Theorem 2 — Approximate-Tube Clearance Transfer

Let \(\hat p_U(s)\) be an approximate path and suppose a certified bound satisfies

\[
\|p_U(s)-\hat p_U(s)\|_2\le\epsilon_U(s)
\quad\text{for all }s\in[0,H\delta].
\]

If

\[
\|\hat p_U(s)-c_i(s)\|_2\ge d_i+\epsilon_U(s)
\quad\text{for every }i,s,
\]

then \(\|p_U(s)-c_i(s)\|_2\ge d_i\) for every \(i,s\).

**Proof.** By the reverse triangle inequality,

\[
\|p_U-c_i\|_2\ge\|\hat p_U-c_i\|_2-\|p_U-\hat p_U\|_2
\ge d_i+\epsilon_U-\epsilon_U=d_i.
\]

∎

**Novelty boundary.** The implication is elementary. The unresolved object is a tight, causal, computationally cheap, certified \(\epsilon_U(s)\). A neural output alone does not satisfy the premise. Split conformal calibration can provide marginal probabilistic coverage under exchangeability, not a deterministic pathwise tube under arbitrary feedback shift.

## 6. Theorem 3 — Certified Coarse Rejection

Suppose a coarse path \(\hat p_U(t_m)\) has a deterministic pointwise error bound

\[
\|p_U(t_m)-\hat p_U(t_m)\|_2\le\epsilon_{U,m}.
\]

Define an **upper bound** on true clearance at that point:

\[
\overline c_{U,m,i}=\|\hat p_U(t_m)-c_i(t_m)\|_2+\epsilon_{U,m}-d_i.
\]

If \(\overline c_{U,m,i}<0\) for any \((m,i)\), then \(U\) is truly unsafe and may be discarded without deleting a safe candidate.

**Proof.** The triangle inequality gives

\[
\|p_U-c_i\|_2-d_i\le
\|\hat p_U-c_i\|_2+\|p_U-\hat p_U\|_2-d_i
\le\overline c_{U,m,i}<0.
\]

Thus the true path is in collision at \(t_m\). ∎

**Important correction.** A lower bound

\[
\underline c=\|\hat p-c\|-\epsilon-d
\]

supports conservative **acceptance** when \(\underline c\ge0\); it does not support safe rejection when negative. Rejecting on a negative lower bound can prune a truly safe narrow-corridor candidate.

## 7. Theorem 4 — Terminal Backup Recursive Feasibility

Assume A2–A4 and A6. If at every receding-horizon decision the selected trajectory is horizon-safe and its terminal state belongs to \(\mathcal C_B\), then after executing its first control, a feasible shifted sequence exists: the unexecuted suffix followed by the backup controller. Consequently the filter is recursively feasible as long as the obstacle assumptions and backup invariance remain valid.

**Proof sketch.** The previously certified suffix remains safe under deterministic model consistency. At its terminal state, \(\kappa_B\) keeps the state in \(\mathcal C_B\). Concatenation yields a feasible candidate at the next decision. Induction gives recursive feasibility. ∎

**Novelty boundary.** This is the standard MPC terminal-set/backup-controller argument. History-guided proposals can improve the chance of finding a better candidate but do not change the proof.

## 8. Theorem 5 — Conditional No-Freeze Progress

If every selected horizon satisfies

\[
V_g(x_{t+H})\le V_g(x_t)-\Delta_V,\qquad\Delta_V>0,
\]

whenever \(x_t\) lies outside a goal neighborhood, then an infinite sequence of zero-progress horizons is impossible.

**Proof.** Since \(V_g\ge0\), after \(N\) such horizons,

\[
V_g(x_{t+NH})\le V_g(x_t)-N\Delta_V.
\]

For \(N>V_g(x_t)/\Delta_V\), the right side is negative, a contradiction. ∎

**Boundary.** The theorem is conditional on existence of a trajectory satisfying both safety and progress. A cul-de-sac or narrow passage can make this set empty; safety must then dominate and fallback/freeze is possible.

## 9. Theorem 6 — Candidate-Set Energy Optimality

Let

\[
\mathcal Q_t=Q_t\cap\mathcal T_{\rm cert}(x_t)\cap\mathcal T_{\rm prog}(x_t).
\]

The lexicographic selector

\[
U_t^*\in\arg\min_{U\in\mathcal Q_t}J_H(U)
\]

has minimum evaluated rollout-plus-terminal upper energy **within \(\mathcal Q_t\)**.

This is exact by the definition of argmin. It is candidate-set optimality, not global trajectory optimality and not a guarantee on actual post-horizon energy unless the terminal upper model has the stated coverage.

## 10. Theorem 7 — Epsilon-Cover Energy Bound

Let the compact feasible set \(\mathcal F_t\) contain an optimizer \(U^{\rm opt}\). If \(Q_\epsilon\subseteq\mathcal F_t\) is an \(\epsilon\)-cover of \(\mathcal F_t\) and \(J_H\) is \(L_J\)-Lipschitz, then

\[
J_H(U_{\rm selected})-J_H(U^{\rm opt})\le L_J\epsilon.
\]

**Proof.** The cover contains \(\tilde U\) with \(\|\tilde U-U^{\rm opt}\|\le\epsilon\). Lipschitz continuity gives \(J_H(\tilde U)\le J_H(U^{\rm opt})+L_J\epsilon\). Since the selector minimizes over \(Q_\epsilon\), its cost is no larger than \(J_H(\tilde U)\). ∎

**Boundary.** Random MPPI-like samples do not automatically form a deterministic \(\epsilon\)-cover. A narrow feasible set can have arbitrarily small sampling probability. A probabilistic version requires a lower density bound and robust feasible volume; learned proposal concentration can reduce rather than improve global coverage.

## 11. Role Separation

- **History:** changes proposal concentration, residual diagnostics, and motion estimation; it is not part of the deterministic certificate.
- **Motion vectors:** physically derived causal features for closing speed, TTC, stopping distance, sampling delay, HOCBF slack, and feasibility margin.
- **Learning:** proposal generation only.
- **Physics:** maps candidate controls to candidate states and realized energy.
- **CBF/HOCBF:** candidate verification or proposal guidance; the exact quartic verifier is the final static-sphere certificate.
- **CLF:** secondary progress admissibility after safety.
- **Energy:** tertiary ranking after safety and progress.

## 12. Boundaries and Non-Claims

- No deterministic safety claim under unknown obstacle motion, LiDAR dropout, or unbounded perception error.
- No global optimality from a finite candidate set.
- No recursive feasibility without a valid terminal backup set and matching dynamics.
- No theorem that longer history improves recall or latency.
- No theorem that a learned proposal is safe.
- No claim that conformal path errors remain calibrated under arbitrary closed-loop distribution shift.
- No novelty claim for generic sampling MPC plus CBF/reachability verification.

## 13. Open Risks

The strongest unresolved research object is a non-vacuous causal tube \(\epsilon_t(s)\) that is tighter than a global worst-case bound and still certified under feedback. Without such an object, the present contribution is an engineering architecture with correct modular safety semantics rather than a new safety theorem.
