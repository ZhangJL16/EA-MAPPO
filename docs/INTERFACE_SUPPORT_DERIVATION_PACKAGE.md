# Interface-Support Derivation Package

## Target

Derive a coherent theory line for compositional long-horizon resource prediction
under a learned nominal policy and an optimization-based safety operator.

The target is not to prove that observing each policy and each filter separately is
sufficient. The target is to characterize when a previously unseen policy-filter
pair has an identifiable Energy-to-Go distribution through a shared
executed-action interface.

## Status

**COHERENT AFTER REFRAMING / EXTRA ASSUMPTION**

The broad statement “component coverage implies unseen-composition
identifiability” is false. The corrected statement requires support coverage of the
state-executed-action interface induced by the target composition.

## Invariant Object

The invariant object is the target hitting-cost distribution

\[
Z_E^{\pi,\Pi,\omega}(x,g)
=
\mathcal L\left[
\sum_{t=0}^{T_g-1}c_t
\middle|
x_0=x,g,\pi,\Pi,\omega
\right],
\]

where the trajectory is generated by a nominal policy, a safety operator, and a
shared executed-action dynamics/cost interface.

## Assumptions

### A1. State and observation

The physical state \(x_t\) is Markov for the plant after including any required
finite-memory actuator, sensing, or disturbance state. The observation channel is
\(O(do_t\mid x_t)\).

### A2. Nominal policy

The target policy \(\pi^\star(da^{\rm nom}\mid o,g)\) is known, queryable, or
consistently estimable.

### A3. Safety operator

The target safety kernel

\[
K_{\Pi^\star}(du^{\rm exec}\mid x,a^{\rm nom},\xi)
\]

is known, queryable, or consistently estimable. A deterministic HOCBF-QP is the
special case

\[
K_{\Pi^\star}
=
\delta_{\Pi^\star(x,a^{\rm nom},\xi)}.
\]

### A4. Shared executed-action interface

For the policy-filter family under study, the physical dynamics and realized cost
factor through

\[
P_\omega(dx',dc\mid x,u^{\rm exec}).
\]

Changing obstacle geometry may change \(\xi\), the filter output, and occupancy,
but does not silently change \(P_\omega\). Wind, payload, or battery-aging changes
must be represented by \(\omega\) and are outside the shared-interface special
case unless explicitly conditioned on.

### A5. Target interface-support coverage

The primitive kernel is identified on the support induced by the target
composition:

\[
\operatorname{supp}d^{\pi^\star,\Pi^\star}(x,u^{\rm exec})
\subseteq
\operatorname{supp}d_{\rm identifiable}(x,u^{\rm exec}).
\]

### A6. Proper stochastic shortest path

The goal is terminal, per-step costs are nonnegative and finite, and the target
executed policy is proper from the declared initial-state set so that \(T_g<\infty\)
almost surely with finite expected cumulative cost.

### A7. Distributional perturbation conditions

Any finite-horizon Wasserstein result additionally assumes bounded costs and a
metric regularity condition for the transition/cost kernels. Infinite-horizon
extensions require uniform transience or an explicitly controlled truncation
error.

## Notation

- \(x_t\): physical Markov state.
- \(o_t\): policy observation.
- \(g\): current goal.
- \(a_t^{\rm nom}\): nominal policy action.
- \(u_t^{\rm exec}\): safety-filtered executed action.
- \(\xi_t\): geometry, perception, and filter context.
- \(c_t\): realized resource cost.
- \(\pi\): nominal policy.
- \(\Pi\): safety operator.
- \(P_\omega\): physical transition and cost interface.
- \(T_g\): first hitting time of the goal.
- \(d^{\pi,\Pi}\): target state-executed-action occupancy.
- \(Z_E^{\pi,\Pi,\omega}\): Energy-to-Go distribution.

## Derivation Strategy

1. Factor the complete trajectory law through the executed-action interface.
2. Integrate policy and safety kernels to obtain the target executed closed-loop
   kernel.
3. Show that a known target policy, known target filter, and identified primitive
   interface uniquely determine the target trajectory law on the target support.
4. Push that law through the goal-hitting cumulative-cost map.
5. Construct an observationally equivalent counterexample when target interface
   support is absent.
6. Separate mean-value transport from distribution-level perturbation and quantile
   statements.

## Derivation Map

1. **Identity:** trajectory factorization follows from the generative order.
2. **Identity:** marginalizing nominal and executed actions defines the closed-loop
   kernel.
3. **Proposition:** interface-support identifiability uses A1--A6.
4. **Counterexample:** failure without A5 uses two primitive kernels equal on
   training support and different on target-only support.
5. **Identity:** Energy-to-Go is the pushforward of the trajectory law.
6. **Identity:** the SSP mean transport relation uses resolvent algebra.
7. **Proposition target:** finite-horizon distribution error uses A7 and an
   explicit coupling.
8. **Boundary:** quantile error needs local CDF/density regularity beyond a global
   Wasserstein bound.

## Main Derivation

### Step 1. Complete trajectory law

For a finite prefix of length \(H\), the law is

\[
\begin{aligned}
p(d\tau_{0:H})
&=
\rho_0(dx_0)
\prod_{t=0}^{H-1}
O(do_t\mid x_t)
\pi(da_t^{\rm nom}\mid o_t,g)\\
&\quad\cdot
K_{\Pi}(du_t^{\rm exec}\mid x_t,a_t^{\rm nom},\xi_t)
P_\omega(dx_{t+1},dc_t\mid x_t,u_t^{\rm exec}).
\end{aligned}
\]

This is an exact factorization under A1--A4, not a learned approximation.

### Step 2. Executed closed-loop kernel

After integrating the observation and two action variables, define

\[
\begin{aligned}
K_{\omega}^{\pi,\Pi}(dx',dc\mid x,g,\xi)
&=
\int O(do\mid x)
\pi(da^{\rm nom}\mid o,g)\\
&\quad\cdot
K_{\Pi}(du^{\rm exec}\mid x,a^{\rm nom},\xi)
P_\omega(dx',dc\mid x,u^{\rm exec}).
\end{aligned}
\]

The policy-filter pair need not have generated a complete training trajectory for
this integral to be defined. However, every primitive term must be known or
identified where the target composition evaluates it.

### Step 3. Interface-support identifiability

**Proposition target.** Under A1--A6, the trajectory law and hitting-cost
distribution generated by \((\pi^\star,\Pi^\star)\) are identified even if this
pair never appeared as a complete trajectory generator in the training set.

**Derivation.**

1. A2 identifies the nominal-action distribution for every target observation.
2. A3 identifies the executed-action distribution conditional on the target
   state, nominal action, and filter context.
3. A5 identifies \(P_\omega(dx',dc\mid x,u^{\rm exec})\) at every interface point
   reached with positive target probability.
4. Substitution into the integral in Step 2 uniquely determines
   \(K_{\omega}^{\pi^\star,\Pi^\star}\) on the target occupancy.
5. Together with \(\rho_0\), repeated composition uniquely determines every
   finite-dimensional trajectory distribution.
6. A6 ensures that the goal-hitting cumulative-cost random variable is well
   defined.

The result removes pair-level trajectory overlap, not primitive interface support.

### Step 4. Failure without target interface support

Suppose a target composition reaches a measurable region
\(B\subseteq\mathcal X\times\mathcal U\) with positive probability, while the
training interface occupancy assigns zero probability to \(B\).

Construct two kernels \(P_1\) and \(P_2\) such that:

\[
P_1=P_2
\quad\text{outside }B,
\]

but on \(B\), \(P_1\) reaches the goal with low cost while \(P_2\) enters a long,
high-cost path. Both kernels induce the same observed training distribution, yet

\[
Z_{E,1}^{\pi^\star,\Pi^\star}
\ne
Z_{E,2}^{\pi^\star,\Pi^\star}.
\]

Therefore component identity coverage alone cannot identify the target return.
Additional smoothness, parametric dynamics, trusted simulation, safe target probes,
or calibrated abstention is required.

### Step 5. Hitting-cost pushforward

Define the measurable map

\[
\mathcal H_g(\tau)
=
\sum_{t=0}^{T_g(\tau)-1}c_t.
\]

Then

\[
Z_E^{\pi,\Pi,\omega}
=
(\mathcal H_g)_\#p_{\pi,\Pi,\omega}(\tau).
\]

This identity fixes the distributional target. Direct return regression and
factorized rollout are alternative estimators of the same pushforward object.

### Step 6. Mean SSP transport identity

For a finite transient-state representation, let \(P\) and \(c\) be the target
closed-loop transient kernel and expected one-step cost. Then

\[
V=(I-P)^{-1}c.
\]

For \((P',c')\), resolvent substitution gives

\[
V'-V
=
(I-P')^{-1}
\left[(c'-c)+(P'-P)V\right].
\]

This is an exact mean-value identity under transience. It does not characterize
q95 or q99.

### Step 7. Distribution-level finite-horizon target

For a truncated horizon \(H\), couple the true and estimated rollouts. Let
\(\Delta_t=\mathbb E[d(x_t,\hat x_t)]\). Under an interface coupling satisfying

\[
\Delta_{t+1}
\le
L_P\Delta_t+\epsilon_{P,t}
\]

and a cost discrepancy bound

\[
\mathbb E|c_t-\hat c_t|
\le
L_c\Delta_t+\epsilon_{c,t},
\]

the coupling inequality yields the proposition target

\[
W_1(Z_{E,H},\widehat Z_{E,H})
\le
\sum_{t=0}^{H-1}
\left(L_c\Delta_t+\epsilon_{c,t}\right).
\]

Policy and filter approximation errors enter through \(\epsilon_{P,t}\) after
their induced executed-action discrepancies are propagated through the primitive
kernel. Infinite-horizon use requires a separate truncation term and uniform
transience; it is not obtained by silently sending \(H\to\infty\).

### Step 8. Quantile boundary

A small \(W_1\) distance does not guarantee a small high-quantile error when the
CDF is flat, discontinuous, or has little mass near the quantile. A q95/q99 error
bound requires an additional local condition, for example a density lower bound

\[
f_Z(z)\ge m_\alpha>0
\]

in a neighborhood of \(q_\alpha\), together with a suitable CDF-distance bound.
Without this assumption, report distributional distance and empirical calibrated
coverage, not a theorem claiming quantile stability.

### Step 9. Selective prediction target

Let \(A_\lambda(x,g,\pi,\Pi)\in\{0,1\}\) denote acceptance by a reliability
monitor at threshold \(\lambda\). The practically relevant quantity is selective
risk,

\[
R_\lambda
=
\mathbb E\left[
\ell(\widehat Z_E,Z_E)
\mid
A_\lambda=1
\right],
\]

together with coverage

\[
C_\lambda=\Pr(A_\lambda=1).
\]

The theory should not identify an empirical support score with exact support. A
valid proposition must assume or establish that acceptance implies a local model
error condition, for example

\[
A_\lambda=1
\Longrightarrow
\epsilon_{P,t}\le\bar\epsilon_P(\lambda),
\qquad
\epsilon_{c,t}\le\bar\epsilon_c(\lambda)
\]

along the accepted target rollout. Substitution into Step 7 then bounds selective
distribution error. The learning problem is to construct an acceptance score whose
held-out risk-coverage behavior approximates this implication.

### Step 10. Trajectory-risk aggregation boundary

Per-step uncertainty scores \(r_t\) are generally dependent along a closed-loop
trajectory. Therefore

\[
1-\prod_t(1-r_t)
\]

is not a valid trajectory failure probability without conditional independence or
a calibrated hazard model. If \(r_t\) are valid upper bounds for conditional
failure events, an initial conservative statement can use

\[
\Pr\left(\bigcup_{t=0}^{H-1}F_t\right)
\le
\sum_{t=0}^{H-1}\Pr(F_t)
\le
\sum_{t=0}^{H-1}r_t.
\]

Alternatively, use a trajectory-level score trained and evaluated on complete
held-out rollouts. The product form remains an empirical aggregation heuristic
until its probabilistic assumptions are justified.

### Step 11. Conformal boundary under composition shift

Standard split conformal and CQR require exchangeability for their finite-sample
marginal coverage statements. Weighted conformal under covariate shift additionally
requires a valid or accurately estimated density ratio and an invariant conditional
outcome law.

Policy-filter composition can change

\[
P(E_g\mid x,g,\pi,\Pi),
\]

not only the input marginal. Therefore weighted covariate-shift conformal does not
automatically provide coverage under general composition shift. It is a valid
baseline in controlled occupancy-only shift, while conditional/interface shift
requires new calibration data, stronger transport assumptions, or abstention.

## Remarks and Interpretation

- The executed-action world model is the natural minimal-interface baseline.
- Exact policy/filter composition can be obvious; the research value lies in
  support characterization, uncertainty near support boundaries, distributional
  long-horizon prediction, and sample-efficient adaptation.
- The strongest AI framing is selective distributional prediction under
  compositional closed-loop shift, not UAV battery estimation alone.
- If the generic executed-action model matches every specialized representation,
  the correct result is a minimal-interface finding, not a failed project.
- The HOCBF Jacobian can explain local action sensitivity but cannot substitute for
  target occupancy support or full trajectory modeling.
- History is unnecessary when the augmented current state is Markov sufficient.

## Boundaries and Non-Claims

- No identification is claimed outside target interface support without additional
  structure.
- No global differentiability of the safety QP is claimed.
- No deep world-model convergence theorem is claimed.
- No q99 stability follows from Wasserstein closeness alone.
- No unconditional coverage is claimed under policy, filter, or physics shift.
- No collision-safety certificate is assigned to the resource estimator.
- No claim is made that safety intervention always increases energy.

## Open Risks

1. The generic executed-action world model may already solve the compositional
   prediction problem.
2. Target compositions may leave training interface support too often for useful
   zero-shot prediction.
3. Long-horizon rollout error may dominate any compositional advantage.
4. Policy-conditioned and causal modular world models may cover most of the method
   novelty unless the support/distributional problem is sharply distinguished.
5. A single UAV/HOCBF testbed is insufficient for an ICLR oral-level claim.
