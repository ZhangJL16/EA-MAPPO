# Implications for learning-system evaluation

For agents that acquire evidence by acting, the acquisition protocol is part of the decision problem rather than an interchangeable implementation detail. This concern extends beyond navigation: ReAct uses actions to gather information from external sources, while AgentBench evaluates decision making in interactive environments. These examples motivate attention to feasible feedback protocols; they do not establish that our finite-hypothesis resource theorem applies to language-model agents. The relevance is an operational question: what information can an agent obtain and use before its next allocation of a limited budget? [ReAct](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629), [AgentBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.03688).

Three comparisons should be distinguished. **Execution capability** asks what is achievable when the environment is known, with finite-horizon terminal requirements specified. **Asymptotic exploration price**, in this paper, means the maximal attainable fixed-instance logarithmic regret coefficient—not generic sample complexity. **Feasible feedback acquisition** specifies the joint experiments, their durations and resource debits, when feedback arrives, and which subsequent decisions may condition on it. These distinctions do not assert that existing benchmarks universally use the first two comparisons together. They define a comparison whose logical sufficiency our theorem tests.

Theorem 1 shows that the first two summaries do not determine finite-budget learning risk. Across the two capacities, every hypothesis retains its optimal average execution gain; known-model charger-terminal values agree at the specified endpoint budgets. The maximal fixed-instance coefficient agrees too. Nevertheless, at budget twelve, the smaller-capacity risk lower certificate exceeds the larger-capacity attainable upper certificate, throughout the stated open structural neighborhood. Both measurement parameters are already observable at the smaller capacity. What changes is the availability of a shared-measurement trajectory and its use within a feedback-dependent allocation of the remaining budget. Equality concerns the maximum coefficient, not every instance or the full asymptotic information geometry.

The resulting evaluation implication is concrete: a resource comparison should report its acquisition protocol, not only its execution value and asymptotic summary. Relevant protocol fields include complete safe experiments, preparation/travel and recovery/reset costs, observations obtained per experiment, feedback times, and allowed conditional continuations. To isolate a learning benefit, compare the same hypothesis class, reward laws, initial information, clock convention, and execution comparator, and state the terminal conditions of each certificate or measured policy. A bundling-disabled capacity control then distinguishes the newly feasible experiment from an increase in the counter value alone. These are reporting and intervention recommendations supported by the scoped result, not a claim to have found a universal scalar metric for acquisition geometry.

Optimal risk and realized learner performance must also be reported separately. More resources can make a better finite-budget information strategy available without causing a given learner to select it. The improving theoretical witness commits to complete sorties and adapts subsequent route choices to their feedback. Our frozen horizon-free learner did not show the benefit at budget 4096; the connected allocation solver was dominated there by its coverage and certification schedule. That observation neither overturns the minimax separation nor validates practical resource-aware superiority. Keeping the distinction prevents asymptotic attainability from being mistaken for evidence at a deployment budget.

Controlled sensing already provides the language for action-dependent observations and continuation decisions. Our contribution is the robust resource-realizable comparison failure, together with its certificate—not the discovery that information costs effort or that adaptive sensing matters. The diagnostic separates a surviving asymptotic allocation price from improvement at every old least-favorable prior. The assay-batch realization shows that preparation and cleanup can implement the same phenomenon outside navigation, but is not a second empirical validation. The reader takeaway is therefore bounded and reusable: **matching execution performance and maximal fixed-instance asymptotic price does not certify equal learning difficulty at a finite deployment budget; the feasible feedback protocol must remain visible in the comparison.**
