# Reviewer C — Methodology

## Summary

The theory is falsifiable in simulation, but a physical-UAV validation would still face the paradox that ground-truth labels for rejected or post-commitment actions are unavailable unless one accepts hazardous trials or trusts a simulator.

## Scores

- Methodology: **5/10**
- Confidence: **4/5**

## Positive Signal

The package now states the oracle-label boundary and does not propose to mine forbidden labels from the environment.

## Major Concerns

1. **Direct baselines dominate the proposed method space.** SafeMDP-, SafeOpt-, ActSafe-, conservative-exploration-, and abstention-style baselines are mandatory.
2. **Resource claim lacks a distinct endpoint.** Battery consumption can be measured, but no new causal estimand or rate separates it from budgeted exploration.
3. **Physical falsification is asymmetric.** False-safe declarations can be observed through failures; false-unsafe declarations require an oracle or carefully governed probes.
4. **No deployable positive result.** The lower bound alone does not specify when UAV data are sufficient.

## Exact Counterexample

If all boundary labels are generated by an accurate simulator, safe probing appears to solve the task without spending physical risk. The result then measures simulator transfer, not safety-induced identification from onboard data.

## Fatal Concerns

- None for a negative theory note.
- **CRITICAL for a full ICLR method paper:** no distinct positive method or empirically identifiable mechanism.

## Score-Change Condition

Methodology could reach 6/10 for a robotics paper with a prospective protocol, matched physical budgets, oracle labels restricted to evaluation, and no theory-novelty claim.
