## What survives the attack

The most defensible contribution candidate is not any individual ingredient. It is the precisely scoped composition of:

1. a goal-conditioned Persistent Generator-SAC policy whose complete continuous support is verified to preserve a goal-independent frozen recovery option;
2. an execution-authority-aware Bellman law that treats Generator branches as continuous measures, `kappa`/hold branches as atoms, and fail-closed as no continuation;
3. a pending-task-indexed hybrid invariant covering normal flight, finite recovery, charging hold, verified departure, and same-task resume.

This delta remains provisional because the two closest persistent-autonomy papers already establish the broad lifecycle idea. No separation theorem currently proves that their augmented-state CBF/LDCBF formulations cannot encode the same mode and battery behavior. The optional state-level normal-authority fixed point is also unimplemented, the charging envelope lacks physical calibration, and current artifacts do not show learned-residual or dual-field benefit. The defensible paper framing is therefore a rigorous robust-certificate and execution-semantics integration unless a concrete separation example is supplied.

## Forbidden novelty wording

- No “first continuous shield,” “first recovery policy,” “first safe action support,” or “first teacher-guided safe RL.”
- No “dual fields are necessary”; only the scalar-ordering counterexample and a falsifiable separate-head hypothesis are supported.
- No “Generator-SAC outperforms SAC” from the current unmatched fixtures.
- No “first persistent task safety,” “first energy-aware interruption/charging lifecycle,” or “charging emerges from learned safety constraints.”
